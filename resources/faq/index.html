Program & eligibility
Who is this program for?
People dealing with unsecured debt, such as credit-card balances, medical bills, and personal or payday loans, who want a legal and effective way to respond. We start by helping you understand your rights and where you stand, at no cost.
What kinds of debt do you cover? Does it include secured debt?
We focus on unsecured debts, credit cards, medical bills, and personal or payday loans. Secured debts (mortgages, auto loans) are tied to collateral and are much harder to challenge on the usual grounds, so this program does not cover them. Which debts can and can't be challenged →

Your rights
What federal protections do I have against collectors?
Several. The Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA) limits how collectors can contact and treat you; the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) governs what appears on your credit report and how to dispute it. Together they're the backbone of your defense. See all the laws →
What is the FDCPA, and does it apply to me?
The FDCPA is a federal law that regulates how third-party debt collectors can contact and treat you. It applies to most consumer debts in collection. It covers when they can call, what they must disclose, and what they're forbidden from doing. Read your rights under §1692g →
What are the most common FDCPA violations?
Calling at illegal hours, contacting your employer or family, threatening arrest, using abusive language, and refusing to verify a debt when you ask. Each can be a documented violation. What to say when a collector calls →
Do I have to prove I was harmed to have a claim?
No. The FDCPA creates liability for violations themselves, not just for proven harm. If a collector violates the statute, you may have a claim, and under §1692k a successful claim can carry statutory damages plus attorney fees paid by the collector.
The process
What should I do when a debt collector first contacts me?
Don't confirm or pay anything yet, and keep notes. Then ask them to validate the debt in writing, which pauses collection until they prove it. We have a step-by-step script for the first call. Ask them to validate the debt →
What if I'm not sure whether my collector broke the law?
That's what the free consultation is for. You tell us what happened, and we tell you whether it was a violation, and what your options are.
What happens if I'm sued while you're representing me?

Will this affect my credit?
Engaging an attorney doesn't affect your credit. The underlying debt may already be reported, and how it's resolved (paid, settled, invalidated, or removed under the FCRA) determines what shows up. Your attorney walks you through the likely path before any work starts.

Getting started
How is Credo Legal different from a debt-settlement company?
Settlement companies negotiate balances. A law firm has legal authority they lack: sending validation demands, appearing in court, and representing you in litigation. The economics differ too, settlement firms profit from quick deals; we focus on the right outcome, which may be a settlement, an invalidation, or a defended judgment in your favor.
