    Your Rights Under Federal Law

    The Fair Debt Collection Practices Act creates specific, enforceable rules that collectors must follow:

    • Collectors cannot call before 8 AM or after 9 PM in your local time zone
    • Collectors cannot contact you at work if they know your employer prohibits it
    • You can demand written verification of any debt: collectors must stop collection activity until they provide it
    • Collectors cannot use abusive, obscene, or threatening language under any circumstances
    • Collectors cannot make false representations: including claiming to be attorneys, police, or government officials
    • Each lawsuit can result in up to $1,000 in statutory damages, plus attorney fees paid by the collector

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Our Team is always happy to help.

    What is the FDCPA and does it apply to me?

    The Fair Debt Collection Practices Act is a federal law that regulates how third-party debt collectors can contact and communicate with consumers. It applies to collectors pursuing credit card, medical, payday loan, utility, and other unsecured debts, regardless of whether you owe the money.

    What are the most common FDCPA violations?

    Calling at illegal hours, contacting your employer or family, threatening arrest, using abusive language, and misrepresenting the amount owed. Any of these can result in $1,000 per lawsuit.

    Do I have to prove I was harmed to have an FDCPA claim?

    No. The FDCPA creates liability for violations themselves, not just for proven harm. If a collector broke the rules, you have a claim, even if you can't point to a specific financial loss.

    What if I'm not sure whether my collector broke the law?

    That's exactly what the free consultation is for. You tell us what happened, we tell you whether it was legal.
